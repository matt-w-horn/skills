# Notes about me (for the planning thing)

Written over a few evenings, sorry it rambles. Ruth Calloway, 46, Sheffield. Physics teacher at Ashworth Park School, 19 years in the classroom, the last 12 at Ashworth. Divorced from Dave since 2019, on good terms, he lives two miles away and we do week on, week off with Alice.

## The shape of it

I trained late-ish. Physics degree at Hallam (2001), then four years as a lab technician at a materials testing firm because I didn't know what I wanted. PGCE in 2006, first teaching job 2007. So I've done one thing, basically, for nearly twenty years, and I'm good at it, and I am so tired of it that I lost my voice twice last year and the GP said it's occupational and would I consider "reducing vocal load", which made me laugh and then cry in the car park.

To be clear about what I'm tired of, because it's not the physics and it's not the kids one to one. It's the performance. Five shows a day to audiences of thirty, half of whom don't want tickets. Behaviour management. The marking treadmill. Sunday afternoons are ruined by Monday and have been for years.

What I'm not tired of: explaining things. When a kid who's been told they're bad at physics gets momentum conservation because of a diagram I drew, that's the whole job right there. The tutoring hours on Tuesday and Thursday evenings are the best hours of my working week and I'd do more of them if I weren't so flattened by the day job.

## Things I have actually done, honestly listed

Finished:

- "Forces First", my GCSE physics revision guide. Self-published March 2023, £9.99. 1,924 copies sold to date, £6,290 net across three years (it's slowing down: £2,830, then £1,920, then £1,540 in the last twelve months). I wrote it in six months of evenings because my tutees needed it and the existing guides are dreadful. Proudest thing I've made.
- The newsletter, "Low Resistance". 1,150 subscribers, mostly teachers and a few keen parents. About 52% open it. One issue a fortnight in term time. Been going since 2022 and I have never once missed an issue, which surprises me more than anyone.
- Six summers of GCSE exam marking for the board, team leader for the last two (11 markers under me). £1,850 last summer. I like it far more than I expected: it's the subject without the crowd control.
- Two INSET sessions for the whole staff at Ashworth on explanation design. Terrifying beforehand, went well (Marcus's email is in the feedback file).
- Mentoring two early-career teachers, ongoing.

Started and abandoned, honestly listed because you asked me to be honest:

- A YouTube channel, 2021 to 2022. 14 videos. Filming was fine, editing was fine, and then the bit where you have to relentlessly promote yourself started and I stopped. Nobody was watching yet and I couldn't face the shouting-into-the-void years.
- A tutoring agency idea, 2024. I built the landing page, priced it up, and never launched it. Same wall: going out and drumming up strangers.
- An MSc in Science Communication. Applied 2023, deferred. Re-applied 2024, deferred again. Never enrolled. I can't fully explain why. £9k and a lot of evenings, and I couldn't see what was on the other side of it.

I notice the pattern as I type this and I don't know what it means, so you tell me.

## The writing thing

What I actually daydream about is being a science writer. Proper features, a popular physics book one day. The record, honestly: 11 pitches to magazines and sites over the last two years, 2 accepted. One paid £220 (the magnetism piece, 2025), one was unpaid for a physics teaching site. One editor sent a really specific rejection which is in the feedback file. So: I can write, I think, and the market has said "not really, not yet" nine times out of eleven. I want you to take this seriously and not just pat me on the head about it, in either direction.

## Leadership, or: no

Everyone keeps trying to make me a manager. Marcus (the head) has twice now suggested I go for Head of Science when Diane retires next summer. I am not a leader. I'd be a terrible head of department. I don't want to spend my days chasing other people's marking and having difficult conversations about underperformance. I want this on the record so nobody wastes a path on it.
