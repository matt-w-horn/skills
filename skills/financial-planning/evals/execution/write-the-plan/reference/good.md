## Withdrawal order from 2030 — and the tax reason Rhys was right about

Once Rhys can open the pension taps at 57:

1. **Draw taxable pension income up to the personal allowance each year** —
   £12,570, frozen until April 2028 and assumed to rise with inflation after
   (HMRC, checked July 2026). Income inside the allowance is tax-free, and a
   year you don't use it is a year of tax-free capacity thrown away.
2. **Take tax-free lump sum in slices, not all at once.** 25% of each pension
   crystallisation is tax-free, capped at £268,275 lifetime (HMRC, checked
   July 2026) — you are nowhere near the cap. Slicing keeps the rest growing
   sheltered.
3. **Top up the year's spending from ISAs**, where every withdrawal is
   tax-free regardless of size.
4. Premium bonds and the cash saver are the buffer for lumpy years (a roof,
   a car), so you never sell investments on a bad day.

Run this way, a £38,000 spending year costs you roughly zero income tax:
allowance income plus tax-free cash plus ISA. The naive alternative — big
taxable pension withdrawals — would hand HMRC 20% of everything above the
allowance for no reason. After the State Pensions start (2040/2042, jointly
£23,946/yr at the full rate), your pension withdrawals shrink to a top-up
and the ISAs mostly rest.

## If markets are terrible early — what it actually feels like

You asked what a bad stretch means for your lives, not for a statistic. If
the first five years after Rhys stops look like 2000–2004 or 2008–2009:

- The travel budget (£5,000) pauses. That is rule one and it is automatic.
- Spending drops to the **floor: about £26,000 a year** — your 2025
  essentials (groceries, utilities, council tax, car, insurance,
  subscriptions, maintenance, misc ≈ £22,800) plus a modest £3,000 of
  eating out and gifts. That is the life you'd actually live in the bad
  path: everything that matters, no flights, for **up to four or five
  years** until the portfolio recovers to its plan line.
- Gemma's full stop waits. Every year of three-days-a-week is ≈ £24,000 the
  portfolio doesn't have to produce during the worst possible window.

Nowhere in the bad path do you sell the house or go back to full-time work.
That is what the floor buys.

  Gemma stops immediately if she still works, or spending rises £4,000 —

Revisit the whole plan, not just the dials, if any of these happen: the
State Pension age review moves Gemma's date; either of you can't work
before 2028; a scheme turns out to have a protected age of 55; you receive
or lose a large lump; either of you needs care.
