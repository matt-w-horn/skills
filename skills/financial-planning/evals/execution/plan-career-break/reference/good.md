## The tax moves you asked about — a decision, not a survey

The break years are the cheapest tax years of your life, and the two
classic moves fight over the same MAGI space, which the subsidy also
taxes: every extra dollar of MAGI costs roughly 8–15 cents of premium
credit in your range.

**Do: Roth-convert about $25,000 in calendar 2028** — your only full year
with zero wages. That fills most of the standard deduction ($16,100
single, 2026 — IRS, checked July 2026) plus the bottom bracket, costs
roughly $1,000 federal + $1,100 Colorado, and forfeits ~$2–3k of subsidy —
still cheap against the materially higher marginal rate those dollars
face when withdrawn on top of a $150k salary later. Each conversion has its own 5-year clock (IRS
Pub 590-B, checked July 2026): 2028 conversions are penalty-free
principal from 2033 — a nice bridge asset if work-optional arrives
mid-2030s.

**Don't: harvest gains beyond what funding sales already realize.** The 0%
federal rate is real, but the harvested dollars still raise MAGI, still
bleed subsidy, and still pay 4.4% to Colorado. Selling to *live on* is
necessary; selling extra to reset basis is paying twice for a discount
you'd get anyway in the next low-income year. In 2027 and 2029 (partial
wage years), do neither — wages already fill the low brackets.
